## Why "Krumb"

The name comes from the German *Krume* — the soft inner of a bread loaf,
distinct from the crust. The crumb is what you actually eat: the part
that nourishes, the part that holds the moisture. The crust holds the
loaf together; the crumb is the loaf.

> *"Feast upon the words of Christ; for behold, the words of Christ will
> tell you all things what ye should do."* — 2 Nephi 32:3

Scripture is the bread of the Word, and this app is for the part you
sit with — the soft middle.

---

## What this is for

A scripture-thinking workspace. Not a generator. Not an oracle. A clean
desk.

The work of preparing a talk, or a lesson, or just thinking carefully
about a verse — it has a real shape: gather, filter, arrange, enrich.
The hard part isn't any of those steps. The hard part is the time left
over after them, where revelation has room to do its work.

This app exists to compress the friction of *gathering* and
*rearranging* so that the time left over is for *pondering*. The Spirit
does the sequencing. Krumb just clears the desk.

It deliberately does not generate talks, suggest the order of items in a
stack, auto-cluster verses into themes, or nag you to "ponder" on a
streak. Those are all tempting and all wrong. Inspiration is
non-systematizable.

---

## The four stages

**Brainstorm.** Drop scraps in. Voice memo, quick text, half a thought.
No structure required. The flour and the water and the salt — you don't
have to know yet what it's becoming.

**Filter.** Search across scripture, conference talks, your own past
notes. Keep what resonates, discard the rest. This is the kneading:
nothing new is being added, but the dough is changing.

**Organize.** Drag the kept items into a sequence. The app does not
suggest order. This is where revelation does its work — *distilling*, in
the language of D&C 121:45, *"as the dews from heaven."*

**Enrich.** Tap a verse to read its surrounding context. Tap a citation
to read the full talk. Add your own thought to each item. This is the
marinating — the patient sitting-with, the salt working in.

When a stack is *Bake until done*, it's not finished — it's resting. The
dough has been shaped. The work now is just heat and time.
